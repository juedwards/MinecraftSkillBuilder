# Build a Sustainable House

Age range: 9–11 · Subject links: Science, Geography, Design & Technology

## Learning aims

Students learn how homes can be designed to reduce waste, save energy and work with the environment. They use sustainable features such as natural light, insulation, gardens and renewable energy to design a practical eco-friendly home.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the task the student can:

1. **Build a complete home.** Make walls, a roof, windows and a door that form a safe enclosed building.
2. **Use sustainable features.** Include at least two eco-friendly ideas such as solar panels, a rainwater collector, insulation, gardens or natural lighting.
3. **Choose suitable materials.** Use materials consistently and explain why they suit the house.
4. **Design useful spaces.** Include rooms or areas for sleeping, cooking and storage.
5. **Fit the environment.** Connect the house to the path and include outdoor features that work with nature.

## Task

A family wants a new environmentally friendly home in this open countryside area. Build a sustainable house on the marked plot. Include at least two eco-friendly features that help save energy or protect the environment. Add a furnished inside and outdoor details such as gardens, trees or water collection. Tell me in chat which sustainable features you used and why. Type **finished** in chat when you are done.

## Starter build

Create a grassy countryside scene about 28 blocks wide (X) and 28 blocks deep (Z):

- A flat grassy plot in the centre about 12 x 12 blocks marked with oak logs at the corners.
- A dirt path leading from the front edge of the map to the plot.
- A small pond on one side and a few oak and birch trees nearby.
- A simple unfinished stone foundation on the plot, 12 x 12 blocks, with one short wall section already built.
- A small fenced vegetable patch beside the plot with a composter.
- Mark the player start on the path facing the plot. Mark the task area as the plot and the surrounding outdoor space up to about 12 blocks high.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Completing the house | Incomplete walls or roof. | Mostly complete, with gaps or missing features. | Complete walls, roof, windows and a door. | A well-finished, tidy and realistic-looking home. |
| Sustainable features | No eco-friendly features included. | One sustainable feature included. | Two sustainable features included and clearly visible. | Several sustainable features work together as part of the design. |
| Materials | Random materials with little planning. | Mostly suitable materials, used inconsistently. | Suitable materials used consistently. | Materials carefully chosen to match the eco-friendly theme. |
| Interior spaces | Empty or unclear inside. | Some furniture or one useful room. | Clear spaces for sleeping, cooking and storage. | Thoughtfully designed rooms with extra useful details. |
| Outdoor environment | No outdoor details or connection to the path. | Some outdoor features added. | Garden, trees or paths connect naturally to the house. | Outdoor spaces strongly support wildlife or sustainability ideas. |
| Explaining the design | No explanation given. | Names one sustainable feature. | Explains at least two sustainable features. | Clearly explains how the features help people or the environment. |

## Notes for the assessor

Examples of sustainable features could include solar panels made from dark blocks, gardens for growing food, large windows for natural light, trees for shade or water collection systems. Use the student's chat messages as evidence for explaining the design. Give formative feedback with specific next steps.
