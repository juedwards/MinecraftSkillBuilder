# New York Skyline: Design an Art Deco Skyscraper

Age range: 10–14 · Subject links: History, Art & Design, Maths, Engineering

## Learning aims

Students learn how New York's famous skyline grew in the 1920s and 1930s, when buildings
such as the Chrysler Building and the Empire State Building raced to be the tallest in the
world. They discover the features of Art Deco skyscrapers, including the "wedding cake"
setbacks that New York's 1916 zoning law required so that sunlight could still reach the
streets, and use them to design their own tower that fits into a real city block.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the challenge the student can:

1. **Use setbacks.** Make the tower step in as it rises, like a wedding cake, so the
   street below still gets light.
2. **Build tall and in proportion.** Build a tower that is at least three times as tall
   as it is wide, with the floors stacked evenly.
3. **Add Art Deco details.** Give the building a striking crown or spire, strong vertical
   lines, and decoration such as patterned bands or gold and metal trim.
4. **Choose materials with purpose.** Use a clear palette (for example light stone, glass
   and metal) and use it consistently.
5. **Fit the street.** Put the entrance on the street side, keep the tower inside its lot,
   and make it look at home next to its neighbours.
6. **Explain the inspiration.** Say in chat which real New York building inspired the
   design and why.

## Task

Welcome to Manhattan in 1930! There's an empty corner lot in front of you, and the city is
in a race to the sky. Design an Art Deco skyscraper for the lot: step it back as it rises,
give it a dazzling crown or spire, and add an entrance on the street. Tell me in chat which
New York building inspired you. Type **finished** in chat when you are done.

## Starter build

Create a 1930s Manhattan street corner about 30 blocks wide (X) and 30 blocks deep (Z):

- Two streets crossing at the front-left corner: dark gray concrete roads 5 blocks wide,
  with light-gray sidewalks 2 blocks wide and white crossing stripes at the junction.
- An empty corner lot of exactly 10 x 10 blocks next to the junction, covered in packed
  dirt, with a low oak fence along its two street sides and a gap for the entrance.
  This lot is the student's task; leave everything above it empty.
- Along the rest of the block, a row of brick brownstone buildings 5 to 7 blocks tall with
  stoops and windows, and on the far side of the lot one neighbouring tower about 25 blocks
  tall in light stone, so the student's skyscraper has a skyline to join.
- Some street details: lanterns as street lamps, and a little green square with a bench.
- Mark the player start on the sidewalk across the street, facing the empty lot. Mark the
  task area as the 10 x 10 lot, from ground level up to 45 blocks above the ground.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setbacks and shape | A plain box, or no recognisable tower. | One setback, or setbacks on only one side. | Two or more clear setbacks that step in as the tower rises. | Elegant, balanced setbacks that build up to the crown, like the Empire State Building. |
| Height and proportion | Less than twice as tall as it is wide. | About twice as tall as it is wide, or uneven floors. | At least three times as tall as it is wide, with even floors. | Soaring and well proportioned, with the parts clearly related in size. |
| Art Deco details | No crown or decoration. | A simple top or a little decoration. | A distinct crown or spire and some vertical lines or trim. | A striking, original crown, strong vertical lines and patterned Art Deco ornament. |
| Materials | Random or unsuitable materials. | Mostly suitable materials, used inconsistently. | A clear palette used consistently. | A deliberate palette, such as stone, glass and metal, that makes the details stand out. |
| Fitting the street | Spills outside the lot or ignores the street. | Inside the lot, but no clear entrance. | Inside the lot, with an entrance on the street side. | A grand street-level entrance and a design that responds to its neighbours. |
| Explaining the inspiration | No explanation. | Names a New York building. | Names a building and one feature it borrowed. | Explains the inspiration and how the design uses it, mentioning setbacks or Art Deco ideas. |

## Notes for the assessor

The lot is only 10 x 10 blocks, so a tower that uses most of the 45-block height counts as
tall. Gold, iron and quartz blocks, glass, and light-gray or white concrete are good Art Deco
materials in this game. Use the student's chat messages as evidence for "Explaining the
inspiration". Celebrate creativity: a design doesn't have to copy a real building.
