# Build a Bridge

Age range: 9–14 · Subject links: Design & Technology, Engineering, Maths

## Learning aims

Students understand how bridges carry people safely across a gap. They plan and build a
bridge that is fit for purpose, considering its span, supports, safety features and
appearance.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the task the student can:

1. **Span the gap.** Build a continuous walkway that joins both sides of the river.
2. **Support the structure.** Use supports such as pillars, arches or beams so the bridge
   would plausibly hold its weight across its span.
3. **Make it safe and usable.** Add railings or walls along the edges and keep the walkway
   at least 2 blocks wide, with a step-free connection to each bank.
4. **Choose suitable materials.** Pick blocks that suit a bridge (for example stone,
   bricks, planks or logs) and use them consistently.
5. **Design with purpose.** Show planning through symmetry, a clear structural idea (for
   example a beam, arch or suspension bridge) and details that improve how it looks or works.

## Task

The village on the far bank has no way across the river. Build a bridge from the start
platform to the far bank so that villagers can cross safely. Use the foundations that are
already there if you want to. Type **finished** in chat when you are done.

## Starter build

Create a river scene about 24 blocks wide (X) and 24 blocks deep (Z):

- A grass riverbank at the front (low Z), about 6 blocks deep, where the student starts.
- A river of water about 9 blocks wide running along the X axis, 3 blocks deep, with a
  sand or gravel riverbed.
- A grass riverbank at the back (high Z), about 6 blocks deep, with a small oak village
  house or signpost so the destination is clear.
- On the near bank, an unfinished bridge start: a 3-block-wide stone brick abutment
  and the first 2 blocks of walkway sticking out over the water, with no railings.
- Do not build the rest of the bridge; that is the student's task.
- Mark the player start on the near bank, facing the river, and mark the task area as the
  space above the river between the two banks, from the riverbed up to about 10 blocks
  above the water, including 2 blocks of each bank.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spanning the gap | The bridge does not reach the far bank. | The bridge reaches the far bank but has gaps or uneven sections. | A continuous walkway joins both banks. | A continuous, level walkway joins both banks with smooth connections at each end. |
| Structural support | No supports; the walkway floats. | Some supports, but long spans are unsupported. | Supports are placed at sensible intervals across the span. | A clear structural system (arch, truss or suspension) is used consistently and convincingly. |
| Safety and usability | No railings; walkway 1 block wide. | Railings on part of the bridge, or the walkway is narrow. | Railings or walls along both sides and a walkway at least 2 blocks wide. | Fully protected, comfortably wide walkway with lighting or other features for safe use. |
| Materials | Random or unsuitable materials (e.g. glass floor, sand). | Mostly suitable materials, used inconsistently. | Suitable materials used consistently. | Materials chosen deliberately for strength and appearance, with purposeful contrast. |
| Design and planning | No evidence of planning. | Some evidence of a plan, e.g. partial symmetry. | Symmetrical, tidy design with a recognisable bridge type. | Original, well-proportioned design with thoughtful details that suit the setting. |

## Notes for the assessor

Give formative feedback: celebrate what the student did well, explain which level each
criterion reached and why, and give one or two specific, achievable next steps.
