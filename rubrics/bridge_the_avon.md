# Bridge the River Avon

Age range: 10–14 · Subject links: Geography, Design & Technology, History, Engineering

## Learning aims

Students use a real place to understand why and where bridges are built. They study the
real River Avon gorge in Bristol, where Isambard Kingdom Brunel's Clifton Suspension Bridge
stands, and design their own crossing that suits the site.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the task the student can:

1. **Read the site.** Use the map (river, roads and paths) to choose where the bridge should
   start and end so that it connects to the existing roads or paths.
2. **Span the river.** Build a continuous crossing from one bank to the other.
3. **Choose a structure.** Pick a bridge type (beam, arch or suspension) and build supports
   that suit a wide river crossing.
4. **Make it safe.** Add railings or walls and a walkway or roadway at least 2 blocks wide.
5. **Explain the design.** Say in chat why they chose this type and position.

## Task

You are standing beside the real River Avon in Bristol, built from OpenStreetMap. The
famous bridge here is missing! Design and build a new crossing between the two banks in the
middle of the map. Connect it to the roads or paths on each side, and tell me in chat which
type of bridge you chose and why. Type **finished** in chat when you are done.

## Starter map

Clifton Suspension Bridge, Bristol, without bridges, size 96, scale 5

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Beginning | Developing | Secure | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading the site | The bridge ignores the roads and paths. | One end connects to a road or path. | Both ends connect to roads or paths. | The position is clearly chosen to link the existing routes, and the student explains why. |
| Spanning the river | The crossing does not reach the far bank. | It reaches the far bank with gaps. | A continuous crossing joins both banks. | A continuous, level crossing with smooth connections at each end. |
| Structure | No supports. | Some supports, but long unsupported spans. | Supports suit the chosen bridge type. | A convincing beam, arch or suspension structure used consistently. |
| Safety | No railings; 1 block wide. | Partial railings or narrow. | Railings on both sides; at least 2 blocks wide. | Fully protected and comfortable to use, with lighting or other details. |
| Explaining the design | No explanation. | Names a bridge type. | Explains the type or the position. | Explains both the type and the position with reasons linked to the site. |

## Notes for the assessor

The map is flat, so the real gorge's height is not shown; don't penalise a low bridge.
Use the student's chat messages as evidence for "Explaining the design".
